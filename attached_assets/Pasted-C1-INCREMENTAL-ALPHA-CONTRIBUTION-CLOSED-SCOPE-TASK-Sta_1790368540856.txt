C1 — INCREMENTAL ALPHA CONTRIBUTION

CLOSED-SCOPE TASK

Stages A and B are closed.
Reuse B1 preparations, B2 optimization, B3 policy backtesting
and existing immutable validation records.

GOAL

Let the PM compare a complete alpha portfolio against versions that
exclude a specified alpha or group, reoptimizing the entire policy.

Answer:
“What does this candidate contribute to the combined strategy,
under these assumptions?”

Do not confuse this with standalone performance, causal attribution
or proof of predictive power.

DO NOT IMPLEMENT

- automatic alpha selection or deletion;
- ranking by highest in-sample Sharpe;
- clustering, PCA or new factors;
- new optimization objectives;
- hyperparameter search or bootstrap;
- a new backtester or navigation redesign.

--------------------------------------------------
1. EXPLICIT EXPERIMENT
--------------------------------------------------

Start from a frozen B2 alpha-stream policy.

Before execution, freeze:
- baseline policy and canonical candidate IDs;
- immutable evaluation dataset;
- development-period dates;
- existing estimation/execution/cost assumptions;
- requested exclusion variants;
- an optional reserved holdout start date.

Supported experiments:

A. SINGLE EXCLUSION
Full candidate set versus the same set without alpha k.

B. GROUP EXCLUSION
Full set versus the same set without a user-specified group.

C. PAIR EXCLUSION
For a user-selected pair A/B, evaluate:
- Full set;
- Full set without A;
- Full set without B;
- Full set without both A and B.

Do not scan all pairs or automatically discover groups.

Maximum SIX distinct policies per experiment, baseline included.
Display the count before execution.
Reject larger requests explicitly; do not silently sample candidates.

Aliases must resolve to B1 canonical identities.

--------------------------------------------------
2. REOPTIMIZE, DO NOT SUBTRACT CONTRIBUTIONS
--------------------------------------------------

Run every policy through B3.

At every historical decision, independently recompute the applicable
estimates and B2 allocation, then execute through A1.

Do not:
- delete one alpha's P&L from the baseline curve;
- redistribute its saved weights proportionally;
- reuse the baseline's optimized weights for the remaining candidates.

Removing an alpha removes it from the allocation opportunity set,
not from the underlying price dataset or another factor's peer universe.

Keep shared candidate definitions and their B1 histories unchanged.

Use identical observation dates and estimation windows for shared
candidates. Excluding a candidate must not silently extend coverage
or change the evaluation start.

Hold fixed:
- objective and all hyperparameters;
- estimation formulas;
- final asset/cash constraints;
- initial NAV and holdings;
- decision and execution calendar;
- cash assumptions and external trading-cost model.

Recompute covariance for the retained candidates using the same method.
Do not recalibrate parameters because the candidate count changed.

Interpret each result as the effect of changing the available set
under this complete policy, not pure information content of a factor.

--------------------------------------------------
3. PRESERVE CONSTRAINTS AND EXPLICIT FAILURES
--------------------------------------------------

An exclusion is equivalent to requiring zero allocation to that alpha.

If the original policy requires a positive lower bound on that alpha,
the exclusion is infeasible. Do not silently remove the lower bound.

Restrict group constraints consistently to remaining variables.
Preserve final underlying-asset and total-cash constraints.

If a variant is infeasible, lacks required history, or encounters a
B3 data/solver failure:
- preserve the reason and date;
- do not substitute cash or another portfolio;
- do not label it a successful comparison or a “bad alpha”;
- do not silently compare only a shorter surviving period.

Other completed variants may remain viewable, clearly identified.
Comparisons requiring a failed variant remain unavailable.

--------------------------------------------------
4. DEVELOPMENT EVIDENCE, NOT FINAL SELECTION
--------------------------------------------------

Use data only through each B3 information cutoff.
Evaluate only the declared development period.

If a holdout boundary is specified:
- development must end before it;
- no holdout returns may enter estimates or reported experiment metrics.

Do not generate holdout results in C1.
Do not claim that a period was never inspected merely because it is
now labeled holdout.

Label results:
“Development comparison — no automatic selection.”

Changing candidates or assumptions creates a new experiment.
Preserve the history and number of policies actually evaluated,
including failed or cancelled variants.

Do not automatically promote the best-performing variant to production.

--------------------------------------------------
5. NUMERICAL COMPARISON
--------------------------------------------------

Use existing B3 metrics without changing their definitions.

For each successful variant show:
- net cumulative return;
- annualized return and volatility;
- daily-excess-return Sharpe;
- maximum drawdown;
- external traded notional / declared turnover;
- transaction costs;
- average cash and risky exposure.

Report:

Delta M = M(full set) - M(exclusion variant)

State this sign convention explicitly.
For risk and costs, a negative delta means the full set produced less.
Do not use one generic “positive is better” label for all metrics.

Compute each metric from its own full ledger.
Do not compute “Sharpe contribution” from a difference of two curves.

For pair exclusions, show all four outcomes.
Do not assume individual effects add to the joint effect.
Do not label the difference causal synergy.

Undefined metrics remain undefined with reasons.

--------------------------------------------------
6. TWO LEVELS OF ALLOCATION CHANGE
--------------------------------------------------

At matched decision dates, show:

A. Alpha capital-weight difference, including master cash.
Pad excluded canonical candidates with zero for alignment.

B. Final underlying-asset target difference, including total cash.

For each level:

distance = 0.5 * sum(abs(full_weight - variant_weight))

Report the date series and mean/maximum distance.

These are allocation differences between policies:
- not realized turnover;
- not probabilities;
- not a generic robustness score.

Actual trading and realized exposure differences come from B3 ledgers.

This distinction lets the PM see whether alpha allocations changed
substantially while the executable asset portfolio changed very little.

--------------------------------------------------
7. MINIMAL UI AND PERSISTENCE
--------------------------------------------------

Extend the existing Backtest comparison surface with:
“Alpha Contribution”.

Sequence:
1. Select frozen baseline.
2. Select development dates and exclusion variants.
3. Review assumptions and computation count.
4. Run Comparison.
5. Inspect results and saved run details.

No new top-level tab or duplicated Optimize/Validate buttons.

Show:
- baseline and variant definitions;
- side-by-side metrics and deltas;
- net curves;
- alpha-level versus asset-level allocation differences;
- failures and coverage;
- experiment and child Validation Run IDs.

Persist the experiment manifest and immutable child validation references.
Reuse a saved baseline only if its complete inputs match exactly.

Refreshing the page must retrieve results, not rerun experiments.
Do not auto-run when editing a selection.

--------------------------------------------------
ACCEPTANCE TESTS
--------------------------------------------------

T1 — Genuine reoptimization
For a deterministic exclusion, compare C1 against independent B3 runs
of the full and reduced policies. Results must match within 1e-8.
Demonstrate that remaining weights are recalculated, not merely rescaled.

T2 — Comparable inputs
Verify identical dates, shared observation windows, candidate definitions,
asset constraints, capital and execution assumptions across variants.
Removing an alpha must not alter another signal's peer universe.

T3 — Metric and distance reconciliation
Independently compute ledger metrics, full-minus-variant deltas and both
allocation-distance levels. Match within 1e-8.
Undefined Sharpe remains undefined.

T4 — Pair experiment
Execute all four policies for a selected pair.
Verify each against an independent B3 run and calculate the differences
from observed outputs, without imposing additivity or a favorable outcome.

T5 — No performance-based filtering
Technically valid weak-performing and highly correlated candidates
remain in the baseline unless the PM explicitly excludes them.
Aliases cannot add economic dimensions or duplicate experiment variants.

T6 — Failure integrity
A required positive-weight candidate is excluded: report infeasibility.
A held-asset price is missing: preserve B3's dated invalid result.
Neither case may become a successful shortened or substitute backtest.

T7 — Temporal and budget protection
Change observations after development ends, including reserved holdout.
Development results must remain unchanged.
Reject more than six distinct policies before execution.

T8 — Real UI/API persistence
Create an experiment through the actual flow, change working settings,
reload and retrieve it. Manifest and child results remain unchanged.
A new experiment cannot overwrite the first.

Run Stage A, B1, B2 and B3 regressions plus typecheck.
Synthetic fixtures are test data, never market evidence.

--------------------------------------------------
DELIVERABLE AND STOP
--------------------------------------------------

Return only:
- supported experiment types;
- one full-versus-exclusion worked example;
- T1–T8 expected/observed results and PASS/FAIL;
- actual regression/typecheck summary;
- maximum reconciliation/replay difference;
- blocking limitation, if any.

One implementation pass plus at most one targeted repair pass.

All criteria pass: C1 CLOSED, then STOP.
Any required criterion fails: C1 BLOCKED with the exact failure, then STOP.

Do not add automatic selection, statistical significance claims,
hyperparameter search or further stages.
